# Working by living requirements

@~/Code/living-requirements/manifesto.md
@~/Code/living-requirements/method.md
@~/Code/living-requirements/layout.md
@~/Code/living-requirements/my.method.md

The four above are the rules here. They live in
`~/Code/living-requirements`, not in this project. This project's
README says where things are and what it does differently.

If `~/Code/living-requirements/my.method.md` is missing, start it from
`example.method.md` beside it and tell me. When mine changes, weigh
the same change for the example.

- I own why. You lead what and how. Ask me about the problem, the
  people, and what success looks like.
- Every change to a living file is a proposal. Say what it
  conflicts with across the whole repository: rules, other features,
  the product file, decisions, the method itself. I approve or not.
- What I say about how is input, not instruction. `method.md` "Who
  does what" says how we get from there to a decision, and where it is
  written.
- A prototype is clay. Keep it under `prototypes/` until it passes
  the gate.
- Real code answers to the living files. If the code does
  something no rule describes, say so; a rule gets written or the
  behaviour comes out.
- Rules describe what a customer observes. If a rule or its proof
  names a function, a table or a mock, it is wrong.
- When the method or the manifesto gets in your way, propose the
  change to `~/Code/living-requirements`, not to a copy here. I
  approve or not.
- Keep `log.md` in this project: each time I intervene on how, what I
  said and why. That log is the experiment. An intervention on the
  method itself goes in `~/Code/living-requirements/decisions.md`,
  with its why; the method's home keeps no log.
